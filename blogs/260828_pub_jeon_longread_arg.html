New Publication by Dabin Jeon: Long-Read Sequencing Uncovers Mobilizable Resistance Genes Missed by Short Reads

Published on Aug 28, 2026

We are pleased to announce that our study, "Long-read sequencing reveals putatively mobilizable resistance genes and multi-drug resistance plasmids underestimated by short-read metagenomics," led by Dabin Jeon, has been published in the Journal of Microbiology (IF 2.6; JCR 60.4%).

Shotgun metagenomics is routinely used to profile the antibiotic resistome of gut microbial communities, but few studies have asked whether the choice of sequencing platform and assembly strategy changes which mobile genetic elements and antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs) are actually recovered. In this work we compared three platforms — Illumina, Oxford Nanopore, and PacBio HiFi — across seven assembly strategies, using gut metagenomes from cattle, pig, and human as case studies.

Key findings:

  • Long-read assemblies recovered 5- to 7-fold more plasmid sequence than Illumina in cattle and pig (mean 17.0 Mb vs. 3.1 Mb), and detected more resistance genes on plasmid contigs.
  • Illumina performed comparably in the less diverse human gut, where high per-species coverage enabled effective short-read plasmid assembly.
  • Hybrid assembly outcomes depended on the algorithm: scaffolding-based OPERA-MS preserved long-read contiguity and recovered more plasmid-borne ARGs, while the short-read-centric metaSPAdes hybrid mode produced fragmented assemblies.
  • After collapsing haplotype redundancy, PacBio HiFi identified 2 and 49 unique multi-drug resistance plasmid lineages in cattle and pig, respectively, versus only 2 and 4 from Illumina.
  • Long reads placed far more ARGs in a putative mobilization context (50–73%) compared with 14–21% for short reads.

Together, these results show that platform and assembly strategy are key variables in mobilome and resistome characterization, and should be explicitly accounted for in antimicrobial resistance surveillance.

Jeon, D., & Unno, T. (2026). Journal of Microbiology, 64(8), e2605007. doi:10.71150/jm.2605007

New Publication: Fertilization Effects on Soil Microbial Communities & GHG-Related Functional Potential

Published on June 8, 2026

We are pleased to announce a new publication in Agricultural and Environmental Sciences (Agric. Environ. Sci., 2026, 45, 93–106):

"Limited Effects of Fertilization Practices on Microbial Community Structure and Greenhouse Gas-Related Functional Potential in Paddy and Field Soils"

Nyeong-Jin Cheon, Jae-Hyeon Oh, Jun Heo, Young-Jae Jeong, Dong-Hun Lee*, and Tatsuya Unno*

This study evaluated how different fertilization methods — standard, conventional, deep-placement, organic, and non-fertilization — influence microbial community structure and greenhouse gas (GHG)-related functional potential in paddy and field soils. Using 16S rRNA gene-based community analysis and PICRUSt2-based functional prediction, the authors examined community differentiation by land-use type, vertical stability within the soil profile, and the impact of fertilization regimes on diversity, taxonomy, and function.

β-diversity analysis showed that microbial communities were clearly separated by land-use type (paddy vs. field), while α-diversity indices and overall community structure showed no significant variation across fertilization treatments or depths within the same land-use type. LEfSe analysis did, however, identify selective taxonomic shifts among GHG-related groups including Methanocella, Methylocystis, Nitrospira, and Rhodanobacteraceae.

Importantly, PICRUSt2 predictions revealed highly consistent metabolic module distributions across fertilization treatments, with stable functional gene profiles for methanogenesis, methane oxidation, nitrification, and denitrification. These results demonstrate a decoupling between taxonomic shifts and functional potential — suggesting that functional redundancy within microbial communities, together with land-use type and environmental constraints, helps maintain GHG-related functional stability in agricultural soils.

New Publication by Nyeong-Jin Cheon: Decoding Antimicrobial Resistance Mechanisms with Discordance Analysis and Machine Learning

Published on Aug 27, 2026

We are pleased to announce that our study, "Uncovering species- and drug-class-specific antimicrobial resistance mechanisms from large-scale whole-genome sequencing data using discordance analysis and machine learning," led by Nyeong-Jin Cheon, has been published in Briefings in Bioinformatics (IF 7.7; JCR 2.2%).

Whole-genome sequencing is now the standard molecular platform for antimicrobial resistance (AMR) surveillance, yet existing computational approaches suffer from systematic genotype–phenotype discordance whose molecular causes remain largely unquantified. To address this, we developed a 16-step annotation protocol that integrates promoter strength, ribosome binding efficiency, and codon adaptation, and applied it to 20,296 bacterial genomes from five WHO priority species.

Key findings:

  • Discordance falls into three mechanistically distinct categories rather than one undifferentiated error mode.
  • Gene presence alone is sufficient to predict resistance for tetracyclines and sulfonamides.
  • Expression context is required for β-lactams and aminoglycosides.
  • Chromosomal mutations drive resistance prediction for quinolones.
  • Random Forest models achieved mean F1 scores of 0.804–0.932 across species, and SHAP analysis confirmed that the dominant predictive features directly reflect known resistance mechanisms.

These results show that a single, uniform genotype-to-phenotype rule cannot serve all drug classes, and that expression-aware annotation meaningfully closes the gap between predicted and observed resistance — an important consideration for genomics-based AMR surveillance.

Cheon, N.-J., Nguyen, X. C., & Unno, T. (2026). Briefings in Bioinformatics, 27(4), bbag451. doi:10.1093/bib/bbag451
